## The map is four blocks, driven backwards

The checkpoint is **12 m in front of the spawn**. The finish is **80 m behind
it**. So the whole race is 64 m of boost pad taken backwards, with the pads
pushing the wrong way — a car reversing straight down the strip gets stopped,
turned round, and carried forwards to +46 km/h back at the start block.

Every human on the leaderboard solves it the same way: **wag the nose ±90–100°
across the strip**, so the pads catch the car at an angle and push it *backwards*
along its own heading. That wag is 11.4 of the world record's 15.039 seconds.

The tempting shortcut is ruled out by measurement, not intuition: full lock
settles into a stable 32 km/h orbit with zero net progress, and centring out of
it flings the car sideways at 210 km/h off the strip.

## Where the time is: the attitude at the lift, not its timing

The margin is diffuse. The launch out to the checkpoint is a dead heat — 0.003
across the whole field — the entry is worth 0.017, and essentially all of the
rest is spread across the four swings.

The field already has the *shape*: both pedals down through the crawl, one gas
lift per swing. The world record lifts at 7.09, 8.91, 10.82 and 12.76; this run
lifts at 7.06, 8.89, 10.82 and 12.76 — **the same moments in race time to within
30 ms.** The difference is what the car is doing when the lift happens:

| | heading at the lift | what the pads give |
|---|---|---|
| the field, including the WR | **80–85°** — before the nose is square | **0–6 km/h** |
| this run | **90–105°** — past square | **25–35 km/h** |

Per swing, in detail (heading 0° = nose pointing back up the strip, ±90° = square
across it):

| lift | this run, heading off → on | WR heading | this run, speed | WR speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (7.06) | 71° → 77° | 69° → 75° | 26.9 → 25.3 | 26.2 → 22.4 |
| 2 (8.89) | −89° → **−105°** | −85° → −97° | 30.0 → **55.3** | 29.1 → 39.2 |
| 3 (10.82) | +93° → **+101°** | +83° → +95° | 32.1 → **67.0** | 29.3 → 34.9 |
| 4 (12.76) | −87° → **−101°** | −80° → −89° | 30.6 → **65.6** | 28.5 → **28.6** |

The world record's fourth lift is worth literally nothing to it: 28.5 → 28.6.
That speed, when you do get it, is spent on the way back through square, where
this run's backwards speed peaks at 9.7 m/s against the record's 8.2, and each
swing eats a little more ground in a little less time.

**Nobody needs a new technique here — they need to hold the swing a fraction
longer before lifting.** The record's own numbers say so: moving *its* fourth
lift three ticks later is worth 0.160 to that run.

One thing not to take from this: **"swing harder" is the wrong lesson.** Peak
swing speed correlates 0.02 with finishing order across the field. The humans who
reach high peaks reach them at the wrong point in the swing and throw the speed
sideways instead of backwards.

## The run, off what you can see

Race times. "Square" = nose pointing straight across the track.

1. **Countdown.** Hold gas. Revving earlier or later is worth nothing here.
2. **0 → 1.050.** Gas, straight, roll 7 m forward and take the checkpoint. The
   whole field is within 0.020 here; do not try to be clever.
3. **1.070.** Off gas. **1.090: brake, and keep the brake pressed until the
   finish line.** The car stops at about 1.800 and starts rolling backwards.
4. **1.100 → 3.660.** Reverse in a straight line, past the spawn, up to about
   88 km/h. Wheel dead straight — every 10 ms of wobble is free time lost.
5. **3.660, the moment the rear reaches the first boost pad** (the join between
   the start block and the first pad): **add gas — both pedals down — and start
   flicking.** For the next 2.1 s alternate full-lock left and full-lock right
   roughly every 100–190 ms, keeping the car pointed straight back up the strip.
   The pads drag you from 88 km/h down to about 21; the flicking is what stops
   them turning you round. You cover 20 m.
6. **5.760.** Stop flicking. **Hold one lock** (this run holds left) and let the
   car pivot. That is the first swing.
7. **Each swing, four times, alternating direction:**
   * let the nose come round to **square and a touch beyond — 90 to 105°**;
   * **only then release the gas**, brake still down, for about 250 ms — the pads
     punch you from ~30 km/h to 55–70;
   * **gas back on** and flip the lock the other way, so the nose swings back
     through square while that speed is pointing down the strip. This is where
     the ground is made.
   * Each swing is worth about 10 m and takes about 1.9 s.
8. **~14.350.** The finish trigger is the near edge of the last block. You cross
   it mid-swing at about 32 km/h with the nose 100° across, and there is nothing
   to set up for it.

**The flick in step 5 is load-bearing, not decoration.** Take the same entry with
a straight wheel and the car is stopped 3.4 m into the pads and thrown back out;
it never reaches the second pad. With the flick, the same 2.1 s covers 20 m.

## How forgiving it is

Steps 1–4 are free. Step 5 is a rhythm rather than a set of exact timings, and it
is the most forgiving part of the run. Step 7 is the map: four judgement calls,
one per swing, each of them *wait for square, then lift*. That is the whole
0.391, and it is a cue you can see rather than a clock you have to count.

Measured against the right control — the human world record's own driven tape,
through the identical test of moving every steering event by one to four ticks:

| tape | mistimings it still finishes |
|---|---|
| **this keyboard run** | **41 %** |
| the human world record | 18 % |

**This run is more than twice as forgiving as the run a person actually drove.**
Both are brittle as frozen tapes — most single-tick edits fail — but that is not
evidence about a driver, who steers off what the car is doing. What matters is
that this line does not ask for anything finer than what the record holder was
already holding, on the same device: the world record is itself a pure-keyboard
run, three values, 38 change events.

**Fewer inputs is not more drivable here, and it was measured twice.** Thinning
the tape from 54 events to 30 cost 0.259 *and* dropped survival from 41 % to
10 %; the extra events are not clutter, they are the corrections that keep the
swing on its cycle. A separate robustness pass bought two percentage points of
survival for 0.130 — a bad trade.

**What will take real practice** is the 11-second crawl itself. It is one long
chaotic slide, and the four lifts have to be judged inside it while the car is
already moving badly.

### What these recordings are

Every file here carries **its own** telemetry. Each sample's position,
orientation, speed and velocity direction was read out of the dedicated
server's engine while it drove that file's own input tape, and its steer / gas /
brake bytes come from the tape itself — so opening one as a ghost in game
replays *this* run, and the two channels of inputs a ghost carries agree
exactly. The regenerator's tick alignment on this map was checked against a
recording the game made itself: regenerating that download reproduces it to
0.0005 m, as the mode of five runs, so these records sit on the game's own
physics tick.

Nine of the 116 bytes in each sample are ours and 91 are still the donor
container's — rpm, gear, wheel rotation, suspension and the surface effects,
byte 89 (the ground-contact flag) among them. The car's motion is this run's;
some of the dressing around it is not.
