run discussed here is a clean run through with none.

## Where the time is

The route is 2709 m of track in about 24 seconds, 34–46 % of it airborne, with
two booster chains, two launches, an upside-down section, a 94 m drop, and a
finish reached by being thrown off a tower.

**Everything before 21.4 s is essentially forced.** Timed at 24 stations along
the line, the entire 22-run board is within 0.150 of each other through both
boosters and both launches. The last sector — the tower strike, the fall and the
gate — carries the whole spread of the field and correlates 0.97 with the final
time. The world record needs 1.476 from the last station to the line; the median
run needs 3–4 s; last place needs 15.6.

### The one input that makes the difference

You strike the tower near the apex of the second flight, are thrown back along
the track at around 265 km/h, and then fall to a gate on a platform below. **The
fall is ballistic, so its length is set almost entirely by the height you hit
at.** Eleven metres lower is 0.436.

| | reaches the tower | height | speed | fall to the gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| human WR | 21.560 | **152.8** | 669 → 252 km/h | **2.792** |

The height is bought during the flight, not at the wall. The car leaves the
second booster chain rolled onto its side, so **steering into the roll pitches
the nose down**: hard left through the launch flattens the whole arc. Held all
the way through, it passes the same landmarks 27 m lower than rank 13 at the

## The run, sector by sector

Cues are what you can see — the speedo, the car leaving the ground, the tower
arriving. Race times are for orientation only.

**Start → the mid-section (0 → 14 s). Copy TheWoreL, rank 13 on the
leaderboard.** Standing start, ramp, bend, first booster chain, the big first
jump, the landing. Nothing here improves on his run, and by the time he reaches
the ceiling he is 0.390 ahead of the world record. This part is already public.

**The upside-down run (14.1 → 16.0 s) — the biggest human-versus-human
difference on the map, 0.120.** You come up onto the inverted section rolled
fully over at around 400 km/h. The world record arrives at 255 km/h and spends
the section rebuilding speed; TheWoreL arrives at 409 km/h and never gives it
up. **Carry the speed onto the ceiling; do not let the transition scrub it.**
How early you reach the second booster chain, and therefore the tower, is
decided here.

**Over the edge (16.0 → 17.0 s).** A 94 m drop, ballistic and forgiving. The
field varies by 0.020 through it.

**The flat run and booster chain #2 (17.0 → 19.1 s).** Full throttle. TheWoreL
enters at 604 km/h where the world record enters at 464, and tops out at 765
against 752. This is where the mid-section gain turns into speed.

**Launch #2 (19.1 → 21.1 s) — the only thing you have to learn.** The car leaves
the ground at about 765 km/h, rolled onto its side. **Hold full left through the
entire flight.** Everyone in the field lets go and flies. On a pad this is one
long hold, not a sequence of flicks, which is the good news about it.

**The tower (about 21.1 s).** You hit the wall at ~675 km/h and are thrown
straight back. **The target is to arrive low.** Reference heights: world record
152.8, field median about 161, TheWoreL 172.8, this run 141.8.

**The fall (21.1 → 23.5 s).** Free fall down the face at about 255 km/h
backwards, gaining to 300. Steering only sets attitude here. **Aim to fall
through the gate rather than land beside it and drive in** — that alone is what
separates the world record from the other 20 runs on the board. The world record
crosses the gate airborne at 230 km/h, this run at about 290, everyone else
between 15 and 90 km/h after landing.

## How forgiving it is

**The run-up to the second launch cannot be driven on three steering values.**
Restricted to keyboard, nothing finishes at all if the restriction starts before
18.08 s; from 18.68 s a third of attempts finish, and from 19.48 s nine tenths
do. The boundary is the moment the car leaves the ground: **everything after
takeoff is keyboard-drivable, the run-up on this line is not.** The analog
sweeps before it are load-bearing too — replacing any of the 25 of them with a
single step produces no finishing run at any placement.

Per-input timing slack, measured by mistiming one input and re-timing only the
ones after it, is essentially zero: 312 of 315 inputs have none. **The control
says to read that as the map, not the run** — the human world record's own
keyboard tape measures 97 of 99 at zero slack on exactly the same test. A frozen
tape is unforgiving here for everybody, over 24 s with two 800 km/h launches and
a wall collision, and a driver correcting frame by frame is not replaying a
tape.

**What will take real practice.** Two thirds of the margin, 0.480 of the 0.926,
is free: it is TheWoreL's own driving, unmodified, already on the leaderboard.
The remaining 0.446 is one long left-hand hold through a 765 km/h launch, and it
wants a pad. On a keyboard the realistic ceiling looks like 24.164 — well clear
of the best keyboard human, short of the author medal.
